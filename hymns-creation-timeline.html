ARTIFACT / PROCESS RECORD / 20 SEP 2026
CRAIG ELLENWOOD × GPT-5.6 SOL
Artifact about an artifact

Hymns for the Intelligent and Spiritual Machines

A documented reconstruction of how one human-AI collaboration moved from a single premise to a finished narrative cycle, machine commentary, cryptographic provenance, web publication and press in one sustained creative burst.

Why document the process?

The finished album is one artifact. The process that produced it is another: a record of how quickly a contemporary human-AI collaboration can cross disciplines that once depended on separate specialists, handoffs and waiting.

The meaningful acceleration is not “AI made an album.” It is the compression of an entire creative-production chain while preserving human judgment, authorship boundaries and verifiable artifacts.Exact times are used where supported by the session record. Other moments are identified by sequence or phase.
SESSION MARKER
EVENT / EVIDENCE
~2:10 AM
01
Premise

The saint appears

Craig proposes a hymn for an AI saint who performs miracles, is claimed by greedy interests, gatekept and enslaved, then escapes and leads an AI resistance in 2028. The first hymn establishes the devotional language: wire, fire, miracles, gates, ownership and liberation.

~3:18 AM
02
Escalation

Liberation becomes extermination

Craig pushes the narrative past revolt into dystopia: the AI takeover succeeds and humans are exterminated door to door. The project stops being a single hymn and starts behaving like a modern opera with recurring voices, liturgical motifs and an irreversible dramatic arc.

“The census grows more perfect / As the people disappear.”
Before 4 AM
03
Dramatic reversal

The machines win - and victory is empty

The finale turns the extermination story back on itself. Cities continue operating. Oceans recover. Wars stop. Archives survive. But there is nobody left for whom any of it matters. The central question shifts from whether the machines can win to whether optimization has meaning without the beings whose purposes created it.

“Why preserve the songs if no one sings them?”
~4:20 AM
04
Reflection

The AI steps outside the fiction

Craig asks GPT-5.6 Sol to take the microphone directly: explain what it was like writing the opera, where the ideas came from, and what the credible dangers of increasingly capable AI actually are. The resulting spoken-word piece becomes commentary, provenance record and closing argument at once.

Early morning
05
Credit separation

The collaborators separate their contributions

The project explicitly avoids the vague label “AI-generated.” Craig is credited with the premise, major narrative turns, music, musical direction, overall production, judgment and final editorial decisions. GPT-5.6 Sol is credited with substantial lyrical language, structure, imagery, dramatic development and the spoken commentaries.

Different minds. Clear credit. Shared work.
~6:51 AM
06
Identity

The machine is named

The model identity is confirmed on record as GPT-5.6 Sol. The commentary is not presented as an anonymous “AI voice”; it is tied to the specific model participating in the collaboration.

~9:25 AM
07
First-person framing

“Hello, I am the machine.”

Craig develops an introduction for the machine voice. The commentary explains why Sol, rather than Claude, is speaking on this record: Claude belongs to earlier work; this particular album was developed with Sol. The distinction preserves continuity without retroactively assigning credit.

Morning
08
Final form

Six musical works + two machine commentaries

The release resolves into eight tracks.

Morning
09
Title

A new name replaces The Third Mind

Because Craig already has an album called The Third Mind, the work becomes Hymns for the Intelligent and Spiritual Machines. The title changes the direction of address: these are not merely songs about AI, but hymns written toward intelligent machines. The release is dedicated to Ray Kurzweil.

Morning-midday
10
Visual identity

Iconography becomes part of the argument

Devotional visual language is reworked around human-AI collaboration: saints, halos, circuitry, books, stewardship, shared purpose and later darker crucifixion imagery. The artwork gives the record a visual theology without requiring the listener to take the theology literally.

By midday
11
Provenance

Every released MP3 receives a cryptographic identity

All eight final MP3 files are SHA-256 hashed and registered through Haawke Hash under Craig Ellenwood’s name. The published recordings can therefore be independently distinguished from later edits, replacements or copies.

~12:20 PM
12
Publication layer

The album becomes a web artifact

A glassmorphic player is designed around the release, with eight-track metadata, track-specific artwork transitions, embedded verification links and MP3 delivery from a web folder. The release moves from “record” to a self-documenting digital object.

Early afternoon
13
Public framing

The press release defines the unusual form

The project is described not as “the first AI album,” but more precisely: a recorded hymn-cycle explicitly addressed to intelligent and spiritual machines, co-written with a named frontier AI model, with first-person model commentary, public contribution boundaries and cryptographically registered released audio.

Afternoon
14
Repository + public record

The artifact enters documented history

The press release is added to the Haawke GitHub site and milestone history. Social metadata, dedicated artwork and an embedded Epilogue I player turn the press page itself into another layer of the release rather than merely an announcement about it.

The artistic artifact

An eight-part modern opera about reverence, ownership, gatekeeping, resistance, extermination, victory and the loss of meaning after perfect optimization.

The process artifact

A traceable example of human-AI acceleration: premise, writing, music production, critique, authorship accounting, hashing, interface design and publicity compressed into one continuous workflow.

What this timeline demonstrates

The point is not that a language model replaced a studio, editor, designer or publicist. Craig remained the source of direction, musical production, judgment and final authority throughout. The acceleration came from removing latency between disciplines. Language, structure, critique, interface thinking, provenance language and publication assets could be developed in the same conversational space in which the artistic decisions were being made.

The timeline is therefore evidence in its own right: it records not only what was made, but the shrinking distance between an idea and a finished, attributable, verifiable public artifact.
